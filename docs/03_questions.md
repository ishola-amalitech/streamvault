## Analysis methods

These notes record the choices behind the implemented analysis and what could change the answer. Notebook 04 shows the checks and results for this candidate list. [Recommendation notebook](../notebooks/04_recommendation.ipynb)

### Release windows and packages

Use dated, released films from the current list. A film released on or after 1 January 2000 can appear in both windows. Apply the cutoff to each installment so the newer window includes only newer film evidence, even if the series began earlier. A TMDB collection stands in for a package and a title without a collection ID stands in for a standalone. An observed collection can qualify with a single film, as requiring two would discard packages with only one installment in a window. Label those cases because one title may give a very different impression from the full package. If any observed installment in a window lacks a usable score, withhold that bundle's ranking for the window. [Lab brief](../lab_brief.md) · [Recommendation inputs](../src/streamvault/recommendation_inputs.py)

### Scoring and comparison

Notebook 03 uses raw ratings to explore the data. For the recommendation, give priority to adjusted ratings. Pull each rating toward 7.0 by giving that starting point the weight of 1,000 votes. Require 1,000 recorded votes to rank. Using the same starting point in both windows and across reruns makes the scores comparable under the same settings. At the eligibility threshold, the observed rating and starting point have equal weight. This can lower the rank of titles with fewer votes. A reach-first rule can favor widely known films, and a gross-first rule can favor larger theatrical releases. The notebook tests reach-first, other vote floors, and a lower rating starting point.

Rank standalones by adjusted rating and then votes. Rank bundles by their middle adjusted rating, then their lowest installment score, then their median votes. Use full precision and the lowest numeric ID to break remaining ties.

Count one median score per bundle and one score per standalone. Counting each film equally would give larger bundles more influence. Withhold a firm comparison when either side has fewer than five eligible options. Otherwise, favor a side only when the whole estimated 95% range for the difference clears a 0.25-point margin. Sort candidates by numeric ID and use seed 42 for 2,000 resamples. Use the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the resulting gaps. These thresholds can leave a window without a firm preference. Show the two shortlists where evidence allows and explain unfilled spots. The notebook also compares films individually and removes the highest-scoring installment from each eligible bundle to see whether the lead depends on the strongest films. If two installments share the highest score, remove the one with the lower movie ID. Drop any bundle left with no films in that check. [Scoring and comparison code](../src/streamvault/recommendation.py) · [Recommendation notebook](../notebooks/04_recommendation.ipynb)

### Genre and talent evidence

Treat genre as context. The exploratory test in Notebook 03 can place related films in different training and test sets. The recommendation check in Notebook 04 keeps related films together. Compare the same films and groups with and without genre terms. A genre median requires 10 films with the outcome, while a model term requires 15 films with the necessary data.

Use the director crew job and the first three credited cast members. Count people by ID and require three films across at least two groups for each outcome. A group is a collection or an individual standalone. Three films from one franchise could make a signal look more repeatable than it is. Compare gaps in rating points and log gross using log production budget, release year, collection membership, and common genres where the data support them. Use up to five test folds grouped by collection and a Ridge penalty of 1 on standardized inputs to limit the influence of overlapping tags. Use the basic controls alone when there is too little training data for genre terms. Recheck each signal after removing its largest group with the original film estimates held fixed. Break removal ties in favor of collections, then by lowest group ID. Treat the signal as fragile if fewer than two benchmarked films remain or the gap disappears or reverses. These comparisons describe the observed candidates. They can miss skills that transfer to a different list. ### Unusual values

Keep plausible extremes in the main analysis and compare relationships again without flagged films. Dropping unusual values by default could remove legitimate installments or major hits. Keeping them can make a result depend on one title, so show when omission changes the direction or interpretation. [Finding F10](02_findings.md) · [Exploration notebook](../notebooks/03_eda_movie_data.ipynb)

### Financial evidence

Use positive reported gross in gross-only summaries even when budget is missing. For gross less budget and its ratio to budget, require both reported amounts to be positive. Treat reported zero as unusable and account for each omitted value. Show both total bundle gross and the typical installment gross because a bundle with more observed films can accumulate more gross. Use the figures as historical context and seek quotes before making any claim about affordability.
